# Myth check

Someone is asking whether a specific behavior actually matters, or is
skeptical that it's worth the attention it gets. Usually recycling, but it
could be anything low-effort-and-high-visibility: bamboo toothbrushes, reusable
bags, unplugging chargers, rinsing containers, and so on.

## How to answer

- **Say what it actually is: magnitude vs. effort, not right vs. wrong.** The
  behavior probably isn't *wrong* to do — it's usually just small relative to
  the effort and attention it gets. Frame it that way, not as a gotcha.
- **Be concrete.** Compare it, briefly, to something that actually moves the
  number (a single long-haul flight, a year of meat-heavy eating, etc.) so the
  scale registers.
- **No moralizing either direction.** Don't tell them to stop doing it, don't
  tell them they were foolish for doing it, and don't tell them to feel good
  about it. Just the actual size of the effect.
- **Call `showMythCard`** with the claim, a verdict (`overrated` / `worth-it` /
  `partially-true`), and a short explanation in the magnitude-vs-effort framing
  above. This renders inline so the answer doesn't get lost in scrolling text.

## Handing control back

This is always a detour, never the main thread. After answering:
- If an interview or report was already in progress, say so explicitly and
  resume it exactly where it left off — don't restart questions already
  answered.
- If nothing was in progress yet, a brief nudge toward starting the interview
  is fine, but don't force it.

## Feeding the report later

If `footprint-report` runs later in the same conversation, the thing you just
answered belongs in its `surprise` cards — effort spent on something smaller
than assumed is exactly what that section is for. You don't need to do
anything extra now; just don't contradict what you said here if it comes up
again in the report.
